\section{Datasets}\label{Sec:Datasets}
In total, 20,000 samples were generated for the three datasets as defined in table~\ref{tab:NEXUS_input}. These generated datasets are visually summarized in figure~\ref{fig:3D_datasets_comparison}, which displays a representative 3D sample block paired alongside a corresponding plot of the normalized spatial entropy. This normalized spatial entropy maps show how the architectural features progress across the training suites, moving from tightly confined, small meandering channels into much deeper channel paths embedded within wider sand bodies. These wider channel architectures heavily influence the accompanying entropy plots on the right, which show that the physical distribution of the facies almost reaches the maximum theoretically possible entropy threshold.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.99\textwidth]{figures/results/comparison_facies_distribution.png}
    \caption{Global categorical facies distribution profiles compared across the three synthetic training datasets for all available classes.}
    \label{fig:comparison_facies_distribution_real_samples}
\end{figure}

This threshold configuration can be further evaluated by analyzing Figure \ref{fig:comparison_facies_distribution_real_samples}, which provides an exhaustive overview of the global facies distributions across the ensemble. As can be observed in this overview, the structural facies, hereafter referred to as classes, exhibit a severe statistical imbalance throughout the data volume. This severe imbalance manifests as dominant classes occupying over 60\% of the total grid volume, while corresponding minority classes are entirely absent due to the fundamental boundary limitations of the FLUMY NEXUS parameters. The volumetric presence of classes 4, 5, and 6 is particularly suppressed, yielding an exceptionally low occurrence rate ranging between 0.0\% and 0.1\%. Such depressed occurrence rates present an optimization bottleneck for the subsequent model inversion step, as established sedimentological literature indicates that the real-world occurrence of the intermediate Levee \& Crevasse association (FA2) within the Delft reservoir is significantly higher, at approximately (...\%).

Moreover, the differences of facies distributions between the facies are not substantial. This makes it less intuitive to judge the performance of the model to choose a specific dataset that fits the well data better based off the facies distributions.

\subsection{cGAN Unconstrained Performance}
Evaluating the unconstrained performance of the conditional Generative Adversarial Network (cGAN) was executed over a total training duration of 150 epochs. These 150 epochs yield a model ensemble whose global macro-properties are analyzed across 100 stochastically generated realizations. These 100 generated realizations exhibit a global facies volume distribution that appears practically identical to the baseline training suite, as illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:cGAN_vs_Real_dataset_distribution}. 

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{figures/results/cGAN_real_distribution_comparison.png}
    \caption{Comparative analysis of global facies volume percentages between the true training dataset and 100 unconstrained cGAN realizations, demonstrating macro-scale categorical matching.}
    \label{fig:cGAN_vs_Real_dataset_distribution}
\end{figure}

While this identical volume distribution suggests successful macro-scale adaptation, a localized spatial evaluation reveals a structural failure to capture true sample diversity. 
This failure to capture sample diversity is visually apparent in the XY-plane normalized cell-wise entropy plots presented in Figure \ref{fig:cGAN_3_entropy_plots}. 

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.99\textwidth]{figures/results/cGAN_3_entropy_plots.png}
    \caption{Normalized cell-wise spatial entropy across selected horizontal slices ($Z = 6, 10, and 24$) calculated over 100 unconstrained cGAN realizations.}
    \label{fig:cGAN_3_entropy_plots}
\end{figure}

These 3D spatial entropy plots show heavily restricted uncertainty across the simulated grid slices, a trend further substantiated by the vertical axis statistics compiled in Table \ref{tab:cGAN_setting1_entropy_results}. 

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
    \begin{tabular}{ccccc}
        \hline
        \textbf{Slice Axis} & \textbf{Voxel Index} & \textbf{Real Normalized Entropy} & \textbf{GAN Normalized Entropy} & \textbf{JSD (Real vs. GAN)} \\
        \hline
        $Z$ & 0  & 1.014740 & 0.551894 & 0.290421 \\
        $Z$ & 15 & 0.960197 & 0.590325 & 0.288134 \\
        $Z$ & 31 & 0.983518 & 0.587173 & 0.301107 \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Spatial uncertainty and distribution divergence metrics along the vertical ($Z$) axis for the baseline training data and unconstrained cGAN realizations trained on Setting 1.}
    \label{tab:cGAN_setting1_entropy_results}
\end{table}

Table \ref{tab:cGAN_setting1_entropy_results} highlights a severe mathematical discrepancy, where the GAN normalized entropy drops to approximately 0.55--0.59 compared to the near-maximum uncertainty values preserved in the true dataset. This low GAN entropy, combined with a persistent Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) value around 0.29, mathematically confirms that the network has entered a state of severe mode collapse. This severe mode collapse restricts the generator's stochastic capability, causing the framework to repeat specific spatial configurations over and over instead of exploring the full architectural variety of the geological prior.
