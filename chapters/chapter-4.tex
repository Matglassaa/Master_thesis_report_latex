\chapter{Results}\label{Ch:Results}
The presentation of the results will follow the order of the workflow: First of all the curated datasets will be displayed with the addition of statistical parameters that can be used to evaluate the difference between the samples in the datasets and the synthetic \gls{gan} samples. Secondly, the post training results summarize the results of the hyperparameter tuning, the~\gls{cgan} unconstrained performance to grouped facies classes as well as the \gls{cgan} unconstrained performance when presented with all facies classes. Finally, the results of the optimization and inversion results are presented, after the model was constrained to the Delft Geothermal Reservoir well data.
\section{Datasets}\label{Sec:Datasets}
In total, 20,000 samples were generated for each of the three training datasets defined in Table~\ref{tab:NEXUS_input}. These datasets are visually summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:3D_datasets_comparison} through three different evaluation metrics: a 2D \gls{mds} plot, random 3D realizations, and pixel-wise entropy maps. \Gls{mds} is utilized to project complex, high-dimensional structural data into a clear two-dimensional space by mapping the relative dissimilarities between the 3D grid samples; consequently, similar realizations cluster closely together, whereas structurally distinct volumes are positioned far apart~\parencite{kruskal1964multidimensional, rongier2025atowards}. The clear grouping effect observed per parameter configuration indicates that the different generation settings successfully manifest distinct, higher-dimensional geological characteristics. 

This structural variance is visually confirmed by the random 3D facies plots, which demonstrate that a reduced sand-body extension parameter yields thinner channel architectures and more erratic meandering behavior. To quantify this spatial variability across the ensembles, the right-most panels of Figure~\ref{fig:3D_datasets_comparison} illustrate the normalized pixel-wise spatial entropy computed over 1,000 samples per setting. Setting 2 exhibits the lowest mean voxel-wise entropy (0.66), indicating a more rigidly constrained spatial distribution, whereas Setting 3 displays the highest structural diversity with a mean entropy of 0.71. Together, these values establish a critical baseline for spatial uncertainty, serving as a benchmark against which the generative diversity of the trained \gls{gan} model will be evaluated. The underlying volumetric composition of these configurations can be further evaluated in Table~\ref{tab:comparison_facies_distribution_flumy_samples}, which provides an exhaustive overview of the global facies distributions across each ensemble.

As can be observed in this overview, the structural facies exhibit a severe statistical imbalance throughout the generated data volume. This imbalance stems from the dominant facies occupying over 60\% of the total grid volume, while specific minority categories are heavily suppressed, highlighting a notable limitation of the default FLUMY NEXUS parameters. Specifically, the volumetric presence of facies 4, 5, and 6 is negligible, yielding an exceptionally low occurrence rate ranging between 0.0\% and 0.1\%. Such extreme skewness presents a severe optimization challenge during adversarial training. Because standard \gls{gan} objective functions treat all voxels with uniform weight, the discriminator becomes inherently biased toward evaluating sample validity based almost entirely on the dominant facies. As a result, the generator can get trapped in a suboptimal training loop that refines large-scale features while completely disregarding the structural nuances of minority facies. This statistical discrepancy must be strictly accounted for during subsequent model evaluations, particularly regarding localized well-mismatch metrics.

When transitioning from the detailed 9-class categorization to the upscaled 3-class configuration, the secondary class naturally expands to constitute approximately 5\% of the total volume. Fortuitously, these grouped facies proportions closely mirror the empirical proportions interpreted from the real cored and logged intervals of the DEL-GT-01 and DEL-GT-02-S2 wells (see Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_well_data}). Therefore, while the original 9-class prior suffers from severe generative constraints under default settings, this upscaled interpretation successfully aligns the dataset's global facies distributions with the target reservoir characteristics.

\section{Post Training Generative Results}\label{Sec:Post_training_res}
All the models were trained on 2 NVIDIA-A100 GPU's with each 80GB of unified memory. When trained for 25 epochs on 20000 samples, the excecution time took approximately 3-4 hours.

Based on the metrics from table~\ref{tab:ablation_study} and entropy plots from figure~\ref{fig:ablation_study}, the best model was achieved in model 5 with a~\gls{jsd} of 0.0155,~\gls{msswd} of 0.07986 and with the best voxel-wise entropy value out of all the models (0.79), supported by the 3D entropy plot closely resembling the 3D entropy plot of the setting 2 test dataset. The~\gls{msswd} metric showed all samples decreasing in sliced wasserstein's distance, indicating that the generators are quickly learning to produce samples that have reduced~\gls{swd} compared to the validation dataset. An interesting observation with regards to the \gls{swd} evolution over 25 epochs can be made: All plots show a stabilizing \gls{swd} after 2000 training iterations. From a \gls{swd} metric standpoint, this indicates that the model has stopped improving its samples compared to the validation dataset. 

Moreover, the base \gls{fluvgan} architecture lead to significant mode collapse looking at figure~\ref{fig:ablation_study}. Only after increasing the model depth (double convolutions \& double residual blocks) did the model train properly without mode collapse. Moreover, when varying the discriminator R1 Regularization, applying the penalty every 16 iterations yielded the best result. All other option (no penalty, every 32 iterations and every iteration) all resulted in mode collapse. Finally, model 7 and 8 investigated whether decreasing the number of training samples from 20000 to 10000 and only updating the discriminator every 2nd iteration, yielded any improved results. Where the results from model 8 yielded similar results to model 6, the metrics from model 8 yielded a much higher mean \gls{jsd} of 0.1190 $\pm$ 0.0324. Additionally, looking at the entropy plot of model 8 in figure~\ref{fig:ablation_study}, The plot shows a significantly higher percentage of regions with low entropy values, indicating that most samples produced by the generator after training have similar pixel-wise values.

\subsection{\glsentryshort{cgan} Unconstrained Performance}
To evaluate if features continue to refine during late-stage training, Model 5.1 extended the baseline Model 5 configuration to 100 epochs. Figures~\ref{fig:2D_Z_slices_models_5(1)_and_flumy} and~\ref{fig:2D_XY_slices_models_5(1)_and_flumy} show the mid-slices along the X, Y, and Z axes for three generated samples compared to the test dataset. Both models successfully generate meandering sand bodies with central mudplugs (\ref{tab:appendix_facies_config_overview}), alongside adjacent floodplains and levees. Additionally, the X- and Y-axis plots display~\say{V} shaped mudplug infills and elongated clay deposits. However, the slices also reveal structural artifacts, including closed loops \circled{1}, previously noted by~\textcite{sun2023geological}, and new isolated random voxels \circled{2}. Since 2D visual inspection cannot capture the 3D spatial continuity of these features, quantitative 3D metrics are necessary to properly evaluate model performance.

table~\ref{tab:model_5_epoch_comparison}, provides an overview of the main evaluation metrics used on models 5 and 5.1.  Both models score almost equal on all metrics: the \gls{jsd} metric improved slightly with more training epochs from 0.0146 to 0.0132 whereas the mean voxel-wise entropy decreased from 0.704 to 0.684. Both models show a clear mismatch in unique patterns present in the ensembles with a $\pm$ 30\% increase over the baseline. Furthermore, Figure~\ref{fig:msswd_mds_best_models} shows no significant structural shift in the \gls{msswd} multidimensional scaling. The clustered distributions indicate that the extra training epochs fail to improve the model's overlap with the test dataset, making the two models hardly distinguishable under this metric.

Figure~\ref{fig:model_5_connectivity_distributions} showcases the facies connectivity distributions for both models. The voxel-wise entropy across the horizontal axes also reveals a trend in the original training datasets. Across both the X and Y axes, the normalised entropy varies significantly, exposing a heavy spatial bias within the FLUMY simulations that leads to a non-stationary geological prior. Along the Y-axis, the baseline displays a inverted U-shape; entropy is suppressed at the grid boundaries ($Y=0$ and $Y=128$) and peaks in the center. This indicates a clear channel-belt constraint, where boundary conditions force the simulated river into a central corridor, leaving the edges highly predictable and lacking architectural variety. Similarly, the X-axis exhibits a distinct U-shaped curve with a sharp drop in entropy around slices 20 to 30. This signature points to a fixed sediment inlet or nodal avulsion point, where the river channel is physically restricted to a narrow path before expanding downstream. Consequently, while the vertical depositional energy remains perfectly stable across the Z-axis, the horizontal baseline prior is heavily biased by the deterministic rules of the process-based simulator.

\subsection{High-Fidelity and Multi-Prior Models}
To test the limits of the generative framework, two additional scenarios were evaluated: a high-fidelity model and a multi-prior model. 

The high-fidelity model was trained on the same baseline dataset as before (Setting 2), but it retained all nine individual facies classes instead of grouping them into three \glspl{fa}. Because the nine original classes are highly unbalanced, this scenario tests how well the generator handles severe class imbalances. 

In contrast, the multi-prior model kept the grouped three-class configuration but was trained on a combined dataset containing 10,000 samples from each of the three different geological settings (Settings 1, 2, and 3). This setup evaluates whether the Model 5 hyperparameters can successfully handle a mixture of different geological rules. Both models were trained for 25 epochs, and the results are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:high_fidelity_multiprior_comparison}.

The high-fidelity model is able to generate samples with all nine facies distribution correctly represented in them (figure~\ref{fig:app_facies_dist_high_fidelity_model}). From a visual perspective we can however see a high number of single isolated voxels belonging to the channel lag facies, as well as a mud plug and sand plug facies with bad connectivity and structures that do not fully resemble geologic realistic structures. The pixel-wise entropy plot (figure~\ref{fig:combined_plot_all_channels}) shows a significant amount of rigid structure, indicating that the model lacks spatial entropy and cannot capture the full complexity of the training data. Conversely, the multi-prior model suffers from heavy mode collapse. When presenting the Model 5 hyperparameters with the combined dataset of all three settings, the training process destabilizes. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:2D_Z_slices_models_hf_all_set_and_flumy}, the generator stops producing diverse geological variations and instead outputs nearly identical, repetitive patterns that fail to represent the unique features of the different input settings.

The \gls{swd} evolution for the high-fidelity model shows that performance varies significantly by channel. For channels 0–2 and 6–8, the \gls{swd} metric decreases immediately from the start of training, showing that the model learns these structures well. However, for channels 3 and 5, the metric shows no improvement, meaning the model fails to represent these specific minority classes. Channel 4 decreases steadily over the full 25 epochs, suggesting it might improve further with more training. Meanwhile, channels 0, 2, and 6 maintain low initial values with occasional minor increases.

These outcomes demonstrate that while the Model 5 configuration was highly effective for the 3-class, single-setting case, directly expanding it to the 9-class and multi-prior scenarios does not achieve stable or diverse \gls{gan} convergence. It should be noted that no separate hyperparameter tuning or ablation study was performed specifically for these two complex scenarios. Consequently, while Model 5 provides a strong baseline, these findings suggest that higher-dimensional or mixed-prior datasets require dedicated hyperparameter optimization to prevent destabilization and mode collapse.

\section{Post-Inversion Results}\label{Sec:Post_Cond_Res}The tuned model was inverted using the first 32 metres of well data from DEL-GT-01 and DEL-GT-02-S2 (Section~\ref{Sec:Delft_Reservoir}). This interval provides an ideal testing ground, capturing highly contrasting geological environments: DEL-GT-01 lacks Overbank Mudstone, whereas DEL-GT-02-S2 contains virtually no Levee deposits (Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_well_data}). This asymmetry tests the framework's capacity to adapt to distinct, localized constraints rather than defaulting to a uniform rule. During the initial Latent Space Optimization (LSO), 100 latent vectors were optimized to fit these trajectories (loss curves detailed in Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_post_lso_losses}). The resulting outputs are termed \say{realisations}, representing generative models explicitly conditioned to real-world subsurface observations.

\subsection{Latent Space Optimization}Figure~\ref{fig:post_optimization_f1_scores_distributions} presents the F1-score distributions of the post-LSO realisations. Conditioning accuracy relies heavily on the optimization threshold ($Th$), which dictates the spatial radius (in voxels) around the wellbore penalized for data mismatch. Voxels beyond this radius receive zero weight in the context loss function, enforcing local geological alignment without over-constraining the broader reservoir architecture (Figure~\ref{fig:anisotropic_mask_appendix}).

From figure~\ref{fig:post_optimization_f1_scores_distributions}, it is observable that model 3 achieves the superior overall F1-score distribution, boasting the highest frequency of realisations exceeding 0.8, whereas model 1 yields the lowest performance. Notably, all evaluated configurations exhibit a wide performance bandwidth, with individual F1-scores spanning a broad range from 0.50 to 0.95. This substantial variability in final conditioning accuracy mirrors the behavior observed during the optimization process itself; the corresponding loss curves (see figure~\ref{fig:appendix_post_lso_losses}) display a matching wide bandwidth, where final loss values can differ by up to an order of magnitude between individual runs. This strong coupling indicates that the latent space optimization is highly sensitive to initial vector trajectories, where runs that successfully converge to significantly lower loss minima directly translate into the highest-fidelity F1-score realisations.

Table~\ref{tab:optimization_f1_scores} details the Macro F1-scores, per-class F1 performance, and mean spatial entropy metrics achieved across these varying threshold configurations. The (macro) F1-score distributions of realisations exhibit a positive response to the threshold parameter. Moving from a tightly constrained threshold of 1 (model 1) to a wider threshold of 5 (model 3) yields a substantial increase across all metrics, with the Macro F1-score rising from 0.70 to 0.84. This upward shift is reflected consistently across all three facies classes. The dominant class ($C1$) scales from an F1-score of 0.77 up to 0.90, while the minority class ($C2$), which typically represents the optimization bottleneck due to severe class imbalance, sees its F1-score jump from 0.56 to 0.79. This systemic improvement suggests that expanding the spatial radius of the context loss mask provides the latent space optimization with the necessary information to allow the model to find valid spatial arrangements that honor the hard conditioning points.

For depth intervals corresponding to the dominant sand body deposits (FA1), the ensemble channel probability consistently stays pinned close to $1.0$, translating to an almost perfect overall match accuracy across the realization ensemble. Conversely, the underrepresented crevasse splay and levee deposits (FA2) expose a significant optimization bottleneck; the ensemble prediction probability for FA2 peaks at only $0.7$, with some runs dropping as low as $0.3$ even within intervals where the facies is explicitly logged. The clay deposits (FA3) exhibit intermediate performance characteristics, demonstrating reasonable structural replication and stability across the vertical column. This difference directly mirrors the underlying class imbalance present within the foundational FLUMY datasets, where FA1 is the most abundant facies followed by FA3, while FA2 remains severely suppressed. The dominant volumetric occupancy of FA1 allows the generator to map its structural boundaries with high confidence, whereas the sparse representation of FA2 prevents the latent space optimization from establishing highly confident, deterministic boundaries along the wellbore paths.

Furthermore, analyzing the relationship between conditioning accuracy and ensemble variance highlights a spatial trade-off during the latent space optimization phase. The main takeaway is that as the global F1-score increases via a wider threshold, the normalized spatial entropy ($H_{norm}$) systematically decreases (figure~\ref{fig:2D_XY_slices_at_well_trajectories}). This behaviour occurs because the area under direct deterministic constraint increases radially from the wellbore with higher threshold values. By expanding this optimization zone, a larger volume of the reservoir is forced to adhere strictly to the static well data across the entire ensemble. Consequently, the area characterized by low entropy expands outward from the well trajectories based on the spatial threshold. When viewed in a horizontal ($X$-$Y$) cross-section, this low-entropy zone expands radially and isotropically. However, when viewed in a vertical section where the $Z$-axis is displayed on the vertical axis of a plot, the zone takes on an elliptical, anisotropic shape, reflecting the different spatial scales or weights applied vertically versus horizontally.

the anisotropic behaviour is captured in figure~\ref{fig:2D_XY_slices_at_well_trajectories}: when moving from \textit{Run 1} to \textit{Run 3} and looking at the top part of DEL-GT-02-S2, the region classified as FA1 decreases when the threshold increases, which is a direc effect of the increased threshold leading to the FA2 and FA3 probabilities of each voxel overtaking the probability of FA1. Moreover, the increased threshold clearly leads to \textit{stratification} of the threshold region which further reduced both the global normalized entropy as well as the geological plausibility compared to the geological setting: stratified regions are particularly uncommon in the training datasets. This could further explain the sudden drop in \gls{jsd} fro \textit{Run 1} to \textit{Run 3}.

\subsection{Pivotal Tuning}The severe spatial variance penalty and minority-class bottleneck incurred during LSO underscore the critical necessity of the workflow's second phase: pivotal tuning. Because LSO struggles to achieve perfect voxel-by-voxel matches within a frozen latent space—especially for heavily imbalanced priors—pivotal tuning temporarily unfreezes the generator's internal weights. This allows for microscopic, localized adjustments to forcefully align the output with the well data without relying on entropy-destroying thresholds.

As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:pivotal_tuning_f1_scores}, applying pivotal tuning to the most tightly constrained baseline (Model 1, $Th=1$) leads to a significant improvement of the Macro F1-scores. The mean Macro F1-score surges from 0.70 (post-LSO) to 0.89. Most importantly, pivotal tuning resolves the $C2$ optimization bottleneck: the minority class F1-score leaps from 0.56 to 0.87, easily outperforming the widest LSO threshold. Notably, Table~\ref{tab:pivotal_tuning_f1_scores} also reveals a clear performance plateau. Unlike LSO, where widening the threshold drove significant accuracy improvements, increasing the pivotal tuning threshold from $Th=1$ to $Th=5$ provides only marginal gains (the Macro F1-score rises merely from 0.89 to 0.91). This indicates that once the generator's weights are unfrozen, a minimal spatial context window is sufficient to achieve peak conditioning accuracy.

Although the tightly constrained configuration ($Th=1$) successfully circumvents the global LSO spatial variance penalty—raising the mean normalized spatial entropy ($H_{norm}$) to 0.714—visual structural degradation is already apparent at this baseline level. The corresponding voxel-wise entropy plots reveal the emergence of rigid, unnatural patterns even at $Th=1$. This indicates that the unfrozen generator overfits to the localized constraint zone, causing the spatial structure to shift toward a geologically invalid state. As the optimization mask expands ($Th=2$ and $Th=5$), this destabilization becomes quantitatively pronounced, evidenced by a sharp decline in $H_{norm}$ (dropping to 0.671 in Run 3). Consequently, while pivotal tuning effectively secures deterministic well alignment, the unfreezing of internal weights introduces inherent structural artifacts, revealing a distinct trade-off between absolute conditioning accuracy and localized geological plausibility.
