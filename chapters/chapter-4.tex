\chapter{Results}\label{Ch:Results}
The presentation of the results will follow the order of the workflow: First of all the curated datasets will be displayed with the addition of statistical parameters that can be used to evaluate the difference between the samples in the datasets and the synthetic \gls{gan} samples. Secondly, the post training results summarize the results of the hyperparameter tuning, the~\gls{cgan} unconstrained performance to grouped facies classes as well as the cGAN unconstrained performance when presented with all facies classes. Finally, the results of the optimization and inversion results are presented, after the model was constrained to the Delft Geothermal Reservoir well data.   
\section{Datasets}\label{Sec:Datasets}
In total, 20,000 samples were generated for each of the three datasets as defined in table~\ref{tab:NEXUS_input}. The generated datasets are visually summarised in figure~\ref{fig:3D_datasets_comparison}: a 2D \gls{mds} plot. \gls{mds} is a technique used to simplify complex, high-dimensional data into a clear two-dimensional map. It looks at the differences between the 3D grid samples and plots them so that similar realisations sit close together, while vastly different ones are placed far apart \cite{kruskal1964multidimensional, rongier2025atowards}. This plot clearly shows a grouping effect per setting, indicating the samples of different settings do in fact display varying higher-dimensional differences. This can be visually confirmed by the two random 3D facies plots per setting, showing that a lower sand body extension as input parameter yields samples with thinner sandbodies and more erratic meandering behaviour in samples of this size. The right-most plots show the pixel-wise entropy computed over an ensemble of 1000 samples per setting, setting a baseline for the entropy readings we look to achieve from a diverse synthetic dataset produced by the trained GAN model. This threshold configuration can be further evaluated by analysing table~\ref{tab:comparison_facies_distribution_flumy_samples}, which provides an exhaustive overview of the global facies distributions across the ensemble.

As can be observed in this overview, the structural facies, exhibit a severe statistical imbalance throughout the generated data volume. This imbalance stems from dominant facies occupying over 60\% of the total grid volume, while specific minority facies are heavily suppressed, highlighting a limitation of the FLUMY NEXUS parameters. Specifically, the volumetric presence of facies 4, 5, and 6 is negligible, yielding an exceptionally low occurrence rate ranging between 0.0\% and 0.1\%.  Such low occurrence rates present a optimization challenge during adversarial training. Because standard~\gls{gan} objective functions treat all voxels with uniform weight, the discriminator may be biased to evaluating sample validity almost entirely on the dominant Class 1. As a  result, the generator can get trapped in a suboptimal training loop that refines large-scale features while completely disregarding the structural nuances of minority facies. This statistical skewness must be strictly accounted for during model evaluation, particularly regarding the localized well mismatch metrics. 

When transitioning from the detailed 9-class case to the upscaled 3-class case, the secondary class naturally increases to make up about 5\% of the total volume. Quite by chance, these grouped facies proportions closely match the actual empirical proportions interpreted from the real cored and logged sections of the DEL-GT-01 and DEL-GT-02-S2 wells (see Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_well_data}). Therefore, while the original 9-class prior struggles with severe generation limitations under default settings, this upscaled version accidentally aligns the model's facies distributions perfectly with the target reservoir.

\section{Post Training Generative Results}\label{Sec:Post_training_res}
All the models were trained on 2 NVIDIA-A100 GPU's with each 80GB of unified memory. When trained for 25 epochs on 20000 samples, the excecution time took approximately 3-4 hours.

Based on the metrics from table~\ref{tab:ablation_study} and entropy plots from figure~\ref{fig:ablation_study}, the best model was achieved in model 5 with a~\gls{jsd} of 0.0155,~\gls{msswd} of 0.07986 and with the best voxel-wise entropy value out of all the models (0.79), supported by the 3D entropy plot closely resembling the 3D entropy plot of the setting 2 test dataset. The~\gls{msswd} metric showed all samples decreasing in sliced wasserstein's distance, indicating that the generators are quickly learning to produce samples that have reduced~\gls{swd} compared to the validation dataset. An interesting observation with regards to the \gls{swd} evolution over 25 epochs can be made: All plots show a stabilizing SWD after 2000 training iterations. From a \gls{swd} metric standpoint, this indicates that the model has stopped improving its samples compared to the validation dataset. 

Moreover, the base \gls{fluvgan} architecture lead to significant mode collapse looking at figure~\ref{fig:ablation_study}. Only after increasing the model depth (double convolutions \& double residual blocks) did the model train properly without mode collapse. Moreover, when varying the discriminator R1 Regularization, applying the penalty every 16 iterations yielded the best result. All other option (no penalty, every 32 iterations and every iteration) all resulted in mode collapse. Finally, model 7 and 8 investigated whether decreasing the number of training samples from 20000 to 10000 and only updating the discriminator every 2nd iteration, yielded any improved results. Where the results from model 8 yielded similar results to model 6, the metrics from model 8 yielded a much higher mean JSD of 0.1190 $\pm$ 0.0324. Additionally, looking at the entropy plot of model 8 in figure~\ref{fig:ablation_study}, The plot shows a significantly higher percentage of regions with low entropy values, indicating that most samples produced by the generator after training have similar pixel-wise values.

The voxel-wise entropy across the horizontal axes also reveals a trend in the original training datasets. Across both the X and Y axes, the normalised entropy varies significantly, exposing a heavy spatial bias within the FLUMY simulations that leads to a non-stationary geological prior. Along the Y-axis, the baseline displays a inverted U-shape; entropy is suppressed at the grid boundaries ($Y=0$ and $Y=128$) and peaks in the center. This indicates a clear channel-belt constraint, where boundary conditions force the simulated river into a central corridor, leaving the edges highly predictable and lacking architectural variety. Similarly, the X-axis exhibits a distinct U-shaped curve with a sharp drop in entropy around slices 20 to 30. This signature points to a fixed sediment inlet or nodal avulsion point, where the river channel is physically restricted to a narrow path before expanding downstream. Consequently, while the vertical depositional energy remains perfectly stable across the Z-axis, the horizontal baseline prior is heavily biased by the deterministic rules of the process-based simulator.

\subsection{High-Fidelity and Multi-Prior Models}
To test the limits of the generative framework, two additional scenarios were evaluated: a high-fidelity model and a multi-prior model. 

The high-fidelity model was trained on the same baseline dataset as before (Setting 2), but it retained all nine individual facies classes instead of grouping them into three Facies Associations. Because the nine original classes are highly unbalanced, this scenario tests how well the generator handles severe class imbalances. 

In contrast, the multi-prior model kept the grouped three-class configuration but was trained on a combined dataset containing 10,000 samples from each of the three different geological settings (Settings 1, 2, and 3). This setup evaluates whether the Model 5 hyperparameters can successfully handle a mixture of different geological rules. Both models were trained for 25 epochs, and the results are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:high_fidelity_multiprior_comparison}.

From a visual perspective, the high-fidelity model is able to reproduce a wide variety of the nine facies classes. This is supported by the \gls{mds} plot and the corresponding 3D facies plots (see Appendix). However, the pixel-wise entropy plot shows a significant amount of rigid structure, indicating that the model lacks spatial entropy and cannot capture the full complexity of the training data. This is confirmed by a mean pixel-wise entropy value of \textbf{Add}, which is much lower than the 0.704 achieved in the grouped 3-class case.

Conversely, the multi-prior model suffers from heavy mode collapse. When presenting the Model 5 hyperparameters with the combined dataset of all three settings, the training process destabilizes. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:2D_Z_slices_models_hf_all_set_and_flumy}, the generator stops producing diverse geological variations and instead outputs nearly identical, repetitive patterns that fail to represent the unique features of the different input settings.

These outcomes demonstrate that while the Model 5 configuration was highly effective for the 3-class, single-setting case, directly expanding it to the 9-class and multi-prior scenarios does not achieve stable or diverse GAN convergence. It should be noted that no separate hyperparameter tuning or ablation study was performed specifically for these two complex scenarios. Consequently, while Model 5 provides a strong baseline, these findings suggest that higher-dimensional or mixed-prior datasets require dedicated hyperparameter optimization to prevent destabilization and mode collapse.

\section{Post-Inversion Results}\label{Sec:Post_Cond_Res}
During inversion, the tuned model was conditioned to the well data presented in Section~\ref{Sec:Delft_Reservoir}. The evaluation metrics were computed exclusively on the first 32 metres of well data from DEL-GT-01 and DEL-GT-02-S2, yielding a total of 64 spatial conditioning points for the Macro F1-score evaluation. This specific interval provides a good testing ground because it captures a highly contrasting geological setup between the two well locations. Within this 32-metre window, the wells display completely different facies distributions: DEL-GT-01 contains almost no Overbank Mudstone, whereas DEL-GT-02-S2 contains virtually no Levee deposits. Choosing this asymmetric dataset serves as a rigorous proof-of-concept. It allows us to assess whether the inversion process can successfully adapt to distinct local constraints across different areas of the grid, rather than just learning a single, uniform rule. During the initial Latent Space Optimization phase, 100 latent vectors were optimized to fit the well data. These outputs are referred to as \say{realisations}, as they represent models explicitly conditioned to real-world subsurface observations.

\begin{figure}[htbp!]
    \centering

\end{figure}

As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:post_optimization_f1_scores}, the performance of realisations achieving a tight fit with the conditioning points is highly dependent on the optimization threshold ($Th$). The threshold ($Th$) defines a specific spatial radius around the wellbore, measured in voxels, within which the generative model is penalized for mismatching the hard conditioning data. Voxels located beyond this distance threshold are assigned a weight of zero in the context loss function, ensuring that the inversion process enforces localized geological alignment without over-constraining the broader, unobserved reservoir architecture~(See figure~\ref{fig:anisotropic_mask_appendix}). Table~\ref{tab:optimization_f1_scores} details the Macro F1-scores, per-class F1 performance, and mean spatial entropy metrics achieved across these varying threshold configurations.

The (macro) F1-score distributions of realisations exhibit a positive response to the threshold parameter. Moving from a tightly constrained threshold of 1 (model 1) to a wider threshold of 5 (model 3) yields a substantial increase across all metrics, with the Macro F1-score rising from 0.70 to 0.84. This upward shift is reflected consistently across all three facies classes. The dominant class ($C1$) scales from an F1-score of 0.77 up to 0.90, while the minority class ($C2$), which typically represents the optimization bottleneck due to severe class imbalance, sees its F1-score jump from 0.56 to 0.79. This systemic improvement suggests that expanding the spatial radius of the context loss mask provides the latent space optimization with the necessary information to allow the model to find valid spatial arrangements that honor the hard conditioning points.

To evaluate the conditioning performance down the vertical profiles, Figure~\ref{fig:post_optimization_metrics_across_Z_axis} displays the depth-by-depth ensemble prediction probabilities for each individual facies alongside the comprehensive categorical match accuracy. The vertical profiles demonstrate a clear correlation between the framework's generative confidence and the global class distributions of the prior training dataset. 

For depth intervals corresponding to the dominant sand body deposits (FA1), the ensemble channel probability consistently stays pinned close to $1.0$, translating to an almost perfect overall match accuracy across the realization ensemble. Conversely, the highly underrepresented crevasse splay and levee deposits (FA2) expose a significant optimization bottleneck; the ensemble prediction probability for FA2 peaks at only $0.7$, with some runs dropping as low as $0.3$ even within intervals where the facies is explicitly logged. The clay deposits (FA3) exhibit intermediate performance characteristics, demonstrating reasonable structural replication and stability across the vertical column. 

This stark performance disparity directly mirrors the underlying class imbalance present within the foundational FLUMY datasets, where FA1 is the most abundant facies followed by FA3, while FA2 remains severely suppressed. The dominant volumetric occupancy of FA1 allows the generator to map its structural boundaries with high confidence, whereas the sparse representation of FA2 prevents the latent space optimization from establishing highly confident, deterministic boundaries along the wellbore paths.

Furthermore, analyzing the relationship between conditioning accuracy and ensemble variance highlights a spatial trade-off. The main takeaway from here is that as the global F1-score increases, the normalized spatial entropy ($H_{norm}$) systematically decreases under wider thresholds. Geologically and mathematically, this behaviour occurs because the area under direct deterministic constraint increases radially from the wellbore with higher threshold values. By expanding this optimization zone, a larger volume of the reservoir is forced to adhere strictly to the static well data across the entire ensemble. Consequently, the area characterized by low entropy expands radially outward from the well trajectories, naturally diminishing the overall stochastic diversity and structural variance of the generated realisations in that region (see Appendix \ref{App:Entropy_Thresholds}).
