\chapter{Conclusion}\label{Ch:Conclusion}
The application of \glspl{gan} presents a promising, albeit highly complex, computational pathway for 3D geomodelling. By utilizing synthetic training datasets generated via the process-based software FLUMY, this research evaluated the capacity of a modified \gls{fluvgan} architecture to learn non-linear spatial relationships and replicate the fluvial-deltaic architectures of the \gls{dsst} reservoir.  

Addressing the central research question: \say{To what extent can \glspl{gan} be used to create geologically plausible realisations of the \gls{dsst} reservoir while conditioned to sparse real-world data?}, this study concludes that while \glspl{gan} are able to generate geologically plausible, 3D reservoir realisations that honor real-world well data, so far they are not able to do both tasks with full accuracy. Moreover, the results achieved in this study were preceded by a long and tedious tasks of trying to find the right network and hyperparameter combination to let the model achieve geological plausibility as well as sample diversity. The model’s generative performance and conditioning capabilities also highlight several key outcomes and operational limitations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Geological Plausibility and Generative Capacity:} The properly tuned 3-class \gls{gan} model successfully captured macro-scale 3D spatial dependencies, effectively replicating the dominant volumetric proportions and long-range structural features, such as meandering sand bodies. However, true geological plausibility is bottlenecked by the extreme categorical imbalances inherent in the training data. While dominant facies like point bars were reproduced with high confidence, the model struggled to accurately represent statistically suppressed minority facies (e.g., crevasse splays), occasionally resulting in topological artifacts such as broken mudplugs and isolated voxels.
    \item \textbf{Hyperparameter Fragility and Mode Collapse:} The model stability is incredibly sensitive to hyperparameter configurations. Expanding the model's scope to handle nine individual facies classes or a multi-prior dataset spanning varied depositional rules triggered severe optimization bottlenecks and ensemble mode collapse.
    \item \textbf{Conditioning to Sparse Real-World Data:} Despite generative challenges, the two-step inversion workflow (latent space optimization combined with pivotal tuning) proved highly effective in anchoring the synthetic realisations to hard field observations. When evaluated against the DEL-GT-01 and DEL-GT-02-S2 well trajectories, the conditioned realisations achieved strong localized accuracy scores ranging from 0.78 to 0.92. The generative ensemble retained a reasonable degree of stochastic diversity in the unobserved regions, although expanding the radial constraint threshold around the wellbores naturally diminished broader spatial entropy.
\end{itemize}

Ultimately, while \glspl{gan} offer a computationally efficient method for generating vast, conditioned ensembles suitable for uncertainty quantification, transitioning this framework to full-field subsurface applications will require advanced loss formulations and architectural upscaling to resolve minority topological features without sacrificing stochastic variance.
