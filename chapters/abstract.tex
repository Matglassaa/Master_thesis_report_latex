\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Traditional 3D geomodelling techniques must compromise between computational efficiency and geological realism. While conventional methods struggle to simultaneously achieve high geological plausibility and condition to hard well data, Deep Learning (DL) generative models offer rapid sampling capabilities well-suited for large-scale uncertainty quantification. This research assesses the applicability of a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN), specifically a modified FluvGAN architecture, to generate 3D realisations of the Delft Sandstone Member (DSST) geothermal reservoir conditioned to sparse real-world data. Synthetic training datasets were simulated using the process-based stochastic modelling software FLUMY. 

The results demonstrate that while a properly tuned 3-class GAN model successfully captures macro-scale 3D spatial dependencies and replicates dominant volumetric proportions, its performance is sensitive to hyperparameter configurations. Stable adversarial dynamics were achieved by expanding network depth via double residual blocks, combined with targeted R1 gradient regularization to preserve high-frequency spatial features. Expanding to a 9-class high-fidelity model or training on combined parameter suites triggered optimization bottlenecks and ensemble mode collapse due to severe categorical class imbalances within the training data. 

To bridge this workflow to practical field application, a two-step inversion procedure combining latent space optimization with pivotal tuning was deployed to anchor the synthetic realisations to hard physical constraints from the DEL-GT-01 and DEL-GT-02-S2 vertical well trajectories. This approach effectively matched the asymmetric local observations, achieving mean Macro F1-scores between 0.70 and 0.91 across evaluated configurations. However, geological plausibility remains constrained by topological artifacts—such as broken mudplugs and isolated voxels—in minority facies, alongside a reduction in spatial variance across the conditioned ensemble. This study concludes that while GANs offer an efficient pathway for ensemble-based uncertainty quantification, successful full-field subsurface deployment requires refined conditioning strategies and architectural scaling to resolve fine-scale connectivity without sacrificing stochastic diversity.
