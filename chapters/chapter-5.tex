\section{Interpretations of key results}\label{Sec:Interpretations}
The implementation of the Fluvgan framework  demonstrated highly stable training dynamics throughout the experimental phase. Once an optimal network architecture was established—a process that proved to be relatively straightforward—the generator and discriminator maintained a steady loss equilibrium. This stability is particularly notable given the high-dimensional complexity of 3D spatial data, confirming that the utilized residual block architecture effectively manages the transition from latent space to high-resolution facies models.

However, evaluating the unconstrained conditional GAN (cGAN) reveals a stark contrast between macro-scale replication and true spatial complexity. As demonstrated by the global volume statistics, the cGAN successfully mimics the overall facies distributions of the geological prior, producing structural proportions that appear nearly identical to the training data. Yet, spatial entropy metrics indicate that the model lacks a rigorous understanding of the true complexity inherent in the synthetic datasets. The severe drop in normalized spatial entropy mathematically confirms a state of mode collapse, wherein the network repeatedly generates similar spatial configurations rather than exploring the full architectural diversity of the fluvial-deltaic system.

Despite these generative limitations in unconstrained scenarios, the model excelled during the post-training conditioning phase. The two-step inversion process—combining latent space optimization with pivotal tuning—was highly successful in anchoring the generated realizations to hard well data. Notably, several optimized latent spaces achieved an average structural match of 99\% against the empirical well data. This exceptional conditioning accuracy validates the practical feasibility of using GAN inversion to force mathematically bounded, stochastically generated reservoirs to honor localized, real-world subsurface observations.

\section{Limitations of the current work}\label{Sec:Limitations}
Severe Class Imbalance: The foundational FLUMY training datasets are characterized by extreme volumetric imbalance. Dominant channel structures overshadow intermediate facies (such as the Levee \& Crevasse association), resulting in near-zero occurrence rates for specific minority classes. This statistical suppression inherently bottlenecks the inversion process, as the network struggles to accurately represent structures it rarely encountered during training.

Ensemble Mode Collapse: The cGAN's susceptibility to mode collapse fundamentally limits its utility for comprehensive uncertainty quantification. Because the model fails to capture the full stochastic diversity of the training data, the resulting ensemble underestimates the true geological uncertainty present between sparse data points.

Grid Resolution and Scaling Constraints: The fixed voxel resolution of 128x128x32 restricts the simulated domain to a localized 2560m x 2560m x 32m volume. While sufficient for demonstrating proof-of-concept well conditioning between two nearby wellbores, this grid size cannot encapsulate the entire Delft Sandstone reservoir, presenting computational challenges for full-field upscaling.

\section{Recommendations for future work}\label{Sec:Recommendations}
Full-Field Upscaling and Dynamic Simulation: Research should expand beyond static geological evaluation by scaling the generated volumes to full-field dimensions using patch-based generation or multi-scale GANs. Furthermore, the 99\% conditioned realizations should be subjected to dynamic fluid flow simulations to quantify how the ML-generated architectures impact actual geothermal heat extraction and fluid connectivity compared to traditional geostatistical models.
